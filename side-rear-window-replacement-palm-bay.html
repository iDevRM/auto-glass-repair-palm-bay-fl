What Does Side & Rear Window Glass Involve?

A side window job starts with removing the inside door panel and clearing every piece of broken tempered glass from inside the door, the window track and the cabin. The new glass is bolted to the window regulator, adjusted so it runs smoothly and seals at the top, and the panel goes back on. Back glass is bonded in like a windshield, and the defrost lines and any antenna connections are hooked up and tested.

Why Does Side and Rear Glass Shatter Instead of Crack?

Most door and rear windows are made of tempered glass, which is heated and cooled quickly so the surface is under tension. That makes it much stronger than plain glass, but once it is broken the stored stress releases all at once and the whole window crumbles into small pieces. That is a safety feature, because small cubes are less dangerous than sharp shards. It also means side glass cannot be repaired the way a windshield chip can.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a cracked side window be repaired?

No. Side and rear windows are usually tempered glass, which cannot be filled with resin like a windshield chip. Once tempered glass is damaged it needs to be replaced, and it often shatters completely within days.

How long does a door window replacement take?

Most door glass replacements take about an hour, including cleanup. Back glass takes a little longer because it is bonded in place and the defrost lines need to be connected and tested.

Is a broken car window covered by insurance in Florida?

Comprehensive coverage usually pays for broken side and rear glass, including break-ins and storm damage. The Florida rule that waives the deductible applies only to windshields, so your regular deductible usually applies here.

What should I do right after a break-in?

File a police report if anything was taken, then tape heavy plastic over the opening and avoid sitting on the seats until the glass is cleaned out. Call us for the replacement, and keep the report number for your insurance claim.
